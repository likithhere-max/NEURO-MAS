# NEURO-MAS Phase 3: Statistical Validation

**Read this first.** Everything measured in this document is **synthetic** (random numbers drawn from stated distributions,
generated by `backend/ml/simulation/p3_statistical_validation.py` and `p3_pi_large_check.py`). Synthetic simulation can show
that a procedure behaves as designed *when its assumptions hold*, and how badly it degrades when they are violated in a stated way.
It is **not** evidence that any rule works on patients. No real-data validation was achieved in Phase 3.

| Evidence type | Phase 3 status |
|---|---|
| Synthetic simulation (this document) | Run, results recorded below |
| Real-data model validation | **Not achieved.** Dataset downloads fail with `Tunnel connection failed: 403 Forbidden` (recorded in `backend/ml/validation/results/fetch_FAILED.json`; re-confirmed in Phase 3) |
| Prospective / clinical validation | None exists |
| Live integrations (Gemini, Supabase pgvector, notifications) | Not executed (no credentials / network) |

Reproduce (all seeds fixed; base seed 20261024; Python 3.13.17, numpy 2.5.4, scipy/sklearn from the venv):

```
cd backend
PYTHONPATH=. python -m ml.simulation.p3_statistical_validation --out ml/simulation/results/p3_statistical_validation.json   # ~2 min 12 s
PYTHONPATH=. python -m ml.simulation.p3_pi_large_check                                                                     # 2,000,000 trials per n
```

## S1. Point prediction interval at a single review

Trials per cell: 20,000. Nominal false-positive rate 5%. (This is the *informational* 5% note; the SIGNAL level in the product is
α = 0.001, `point_alpha_signal`.)

| History n | iid Gaussian | AR(1), ρ = 0.6 | iid Student t₃ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 5.09% (4.79–5.40) | 6.11% (5.78–6.45) | 6.42% (6.09–6.77) |
| 4 | 5.15% (4.85–5.46) | 6.56% (6.23–6.91) | 6.70% (6.37–7.06) |
| 5 | 5.08% (4.78–5.39) | 6.93% (6.59–7.29) | 7.07% (6.72–7.43) |
| 8 | 5.11% (4.81–5.42) | 7.46% (7.10–7.83) | 7.38% (7.03–7.75) |
| 15 | 5.06% (4.76–5.37) | 6.65% (6.31–7.00) | 6.89% (6.55–7.25) |
| 30 | 5.39% (5.09–5.71) | 6.12% (5.79–6.46) | 6.29% (5.96–6.63) |

Reading. For iid Gaussian data the rate sits at the nominal 5%. The single exception by interval is n = 30 (5.39%, interval excludes
5%). I tested whether that was a real bias: with 2,000,000 trials per n the rate is 0.0500 (n = 3, 5, 15), 0.0498 (30), 0.0499 (31, 40), 0.0496
(60); the implemented threshold matches the exact `t·sqrt(1+1/n)` quantile to ≤ 4e-4 in all cases. The n = 30 cell was a chance
excursion (roughly a 1-in-4 event across six cells), **not a defect**. Autocorrelation (ρ = 0.6) and heavy tails push the rate to 6–7.5%:
the interval is mildly anti-conservative when its assumptions fail, at a single review.

## S2. Repeated testing: family-wise false alarms of the sequential charts

4,000 series per cell, 40 iid Gaussian readings, no autocorrelation gate.

| Design | CUSUM | EWMA | either |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-starting (Q-statistics), 40 readings | 7.15% (6.39–7.99) | 7.72% (6.94–8.59) | **10.27% (9.37–11.25)** |
| Fixed baseline of 5, 35 further steps | 50.8% (49.3–52.4) | 49.5% (48.0–51.1) | — |
| Fixed baseline of 12 | 31.8% (30.3–33.2) | 30.4% (29.0–31.8) | — |
| Fixed baseline of 30 | 18.5% (17.3–19.7) | 17.5% (16.3–18.7) | — |

Reading.
* The textbook ARL₀ of ~465 for CUSUM(0.5, 5) does not carry over: with estimated parameters the fixed-baseline designs false-alarm
  18–51% of the time over 35 steps. This supports the Phase 2 decision to use the self-starting design.
* The self-starting pair still produces a **family-wise false alarm in about 1 series in 10** (CUSUM and EWMA are run on the same
  data, so "either" is not the sum of the two). The Phase 2 text quoted 6.9% / 7.0% for the two charts individually; they are now
  **recomputed from code** (7.2% / 7.7% here) rather than hard-coded. Phase 2's `rejected_fixed_baseline` numbers in
  `anomaly_study.py` are still literals, and are marked as such.
* The autocorrelation gate (`sequential_max_rho = 0.3`) is a mitigation, not a correction; its effect on real series is unknown.

## S3. Trend rule: Mann-Kendall type I error and power

1,500 trend-free series per cell; nominal α = 0.01 (`trend_alpha`).

| Noise | n | Without correction | With lag-1 correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| iid | 20 | 0.60% (0.32–1.14) | 0.47% (0.23–0.96) |
| iid | 40 | 0.67% (0.36–1.22) | 0.53% (0.27–1.05) |
| AR(1) 0.3 | 20 | 4.00% (3.12–5.11) | 2.40% (1.74–3.30) |
| AR(1) 0.3 | 40 | 4.13% (3.24–5.26) | 1.27% (0.81–1.97) |
| AR(1) 0.6 | 20 | 12.47% (10.89–14.23) | **4.73% (3.77–5.93)** |
| AR(1) 0.6 | 40 | 16.87% (15.06–18.85) | **3.07% (2.31–4.07)** |

Power for a drift of 3 SD in total over the window (iid noise; the p < 0.01 criterion only — the product also requires a minimum effect size of 1 baseline SD, which this figure does not include): n = 20 → 70.9% (68.6–73.2); n = 40 → 98.9% (98.3–99.3).

Reading. iid: conservative as expected for the normal approximation at small n. The lag-1 correction cuts the autocorrelated
inflation by a factor of 2.6–5.5 but **does not restore the nominal level at ρ = 0.6** (3–5× too many false trends). This is documented as
*incomplete* in `MATHEMATICAL_AUDIT.md` §D. I did not substitute another estimator silently.

## S4. Leakage, selection bias and the evaluation split

All on **null data** (labels independent of features; true AUC = 0.5), so any departure from 0.5 is an artefact of the procedure.

| Procedure | Mean AUC (95% CI of the mean across repetitions, mean ± 1.96·SE) | Reps |
|---|---|---|
| Row-level K-fold (rows from the same subject in train and test) | **0.966** (0.961–0.972) | 40 |
| Subject-level (grouped) K-fold | 0.470 (0.436–0.503) | 40 |
| Naive best-of-7-candidates selection, scored on the selection folds | **0.649** (0.614–0.684) | 13 |
| Nested selection (honest estimate) | 0.529 (0.478–0.580) | 13 |
| Leave-one-subject-out, pooled predictions | 0.473 (0.396–0.551) | 13 |

Reading.
* **Subject-level leakage is the dominant hazard.** A row-level split reports AUC 0.97 on pure noise. The repository's split helper
  refuses a row-level split (`leakage_guard_refuses_row_split = true`), and the shipped models use grouped CV.
* **Selection on the evaluation folds inflates AUC by ~0.12** on null data (0.65 vs 0.53). The shipped risk-classifier metadata
  (pooled AUC 0.640, selected between two model families on the same folds) is therefore **optimistic by an unknown amount** and
  no nested estimate was available.
* **Pooled leave-one-subject-out AUC falls *below* 0.5 under the null** (0.473), because the training prior moves against the held-out
  subject. A LOSO AUC cannot be read against a 0.5 chance line without a permutation null. `ml/evalkit/metrics.py` contains a
  group-label permutation test for this.

### What the shipped risk classifier's own metadata supports (no new real-data run was possible)

From `ml/models/parkinsons_risk_classifier_metadata.json`:

| Fact | Value | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Subjects (independent clusters) | **42** | Effective sample size is 42, not the 5,801 recordings |
| Positive class rate | 0.382 | Majority-class accuracy is 0.618 |
| Pooled accuracy | 0.630 | Only 0.012 above the do-nothing baseline |
| Pooled ROC-AUC | 0.640 | No confidence interval was reported; with 42 clusters it would be wide. The internal pass gate is AUC ≥ 0.6 |
| Features include `test_time` and `baseline_total_UPDRS` | — | No time-only or baseline-only reference model is recorded, so any value added by the voice features is **unquantified** |
| Label | Δ total UPDRS ≥ 3.0 from the patient's first visit | The 3.0 cut-off is "illustrative", not clinical (stated in the file) |
| Calibration | `class_weight='balanced'` | Scores approximate a 50/50-prior posterior (S5), not a probability |

This is a demonstration model on one public data set. It supports **no** claim of clinical performance.
**Required before any claim:** cluster-bootstrap CI for AUC; majority-class, `test_time`-only and `baseline_total_UPDRS`-only
baselines under the same grouped folds; nested model selection; a permutation null; external validation on a different cohort.

## S5. Calibration of class-weighted logistic regression

Well-specified model (one feature, 1 SD class separation), large sample. ECE with 10 equal-width bins.

| Deployment prevalence | Plain LR: mean predicted / ECE | `class_weight='balanced'`: mean predicted / ECE | Balanced + prior-shift correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.047 / 0.003 | 0.412 / **0.363** | 0.049 / 0.002 |
| 0.10 | 0.100 / 0.002 | 0.416 / **0.316** | 0.100 / 0.002 |
| 0.25 | 0.247 / 0.007 | 0.447 / **0.196** | 0.249 / 0.005 |
| 0.50 | 0.500 / 0.004 | 0.501 / 0.003 | 0.501 / 0.003 |
| 0.75 | 0.751 / 0.004 | 0.551 / **0.201** | 0.751 / 0.005 |

Reading. Balanced weighting yields scores that are right only when the deployment prevalence is 50%. They can be repaired, but only
with the true deployment prevalence, which the project does not know. Hence these outputs are labelled `probability_calibrated: false`
with an explanatory note (defect D10). Calibration cannot be assessed on the shipped models at all: there are no held-out real predictions in the repository.

## S6. Out-of-distribution / input-domain detector

8 features, 500 training rows in 25 groups, 20 repetitions.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| False rejection of in-distribution inputs | 1.31% mean (range 0.5–3.5%) vs design target ≤ 1% plus range-margin effects |
| Missed: one feature shifted +3 SD | **47.3%** |
| Missed: one feature × 1000 (unit error) | 0% |
| Missed: correlation between a pair destroyed by shuffling | **98.1%** |
| Missed: every feature shifted +1 SD | **75.0%** |

Reading. The detector reliably catches gross errors (units, range) and is a **weak** monitor of moderate or correlation-structure
shift. It must not be described as distribution-shift detection. (A cosmetic issue: the script's `rows` field in this section is an empty list.)

## S7. Agent consensus and shared (correlated) error

3,000 trials. Truth: H1 support 0.55 vs H2 0.45; observation noise N(0, 0.15); two voting agents.

| Condition | Top-1 correct | Reported independent sources |
|---|---|---|
| One agent | 64.3% (62.6–66.0) | 1 |
| Two agents, independent noise | 70.3% (68.7–71.9) | 2 |
| Two agents, **shared** noise (same upstream error) | 65.3% (63.6–67.0) | **2** |

Reading. Agreement between two agents that share an error is no better than one agent, yet the index reports two independent
sources. The index cannot detect correlation that the emitters do not declare. This is the central residual weakness of the
consensus layer; it is pinned by the test `test_independence_is_declared_not_verified__known_limitation` and listed under
unverified assumptions. The index is an ordering of support, labelled `uncalibrated_support_index`, `probability = None`.

## S8. Exhaustive enumeration of the risk heuristic

Every reachable input of the three sub-scores was enumerated (16 fall-risk combinations, 20 seizure-risk, 4 deterioration-risk); the
full table is in the JSON. Facts established: scores are exactly the weighted sums in `MATHEMATICAL_AUDIT.md` §I; buckets are a
monotone step function; the maximum is 1.0; the same *longitudinal deteriorating* flag feeds two sub-scores; `overall = max`. No
sample or population is involved, so no statistical claim is made — this is a correctness check of a deterministic function whose
weights are unvalidated.

| Check | Result |

## Sample sizes and intervals — what exists

* Synthetic: 600–2,000,000 trials per cell as stated, 95% Wilson intervals throughout.
* Real: 42 subjects, no intervals reported.
* Within-patient anomaly layer: no real series have ever been used. False-alarm and detection rates on real patients are **unknown**.
